PRODUCTION NOTES
Internal, documentary project. Not for distribution.

STATUS: Outline locked, narration draft in progress.

Episode spine:
1. Corsica and the outsider (1769 to 1793)
2. The rise: revolution, Egypt, and the coup (1793 to 1804)
3. Emperor of Europe (1804 to 1812)
4. The unraveling: Russia, Elba, and the Hundred Days
5. Waterloo and Saint Helena (1815 to 1821)

Open threads to resolve before we record narration:
- Height. We keep getting notes from viewers-in-mind who "know" he was short. He was not, by the standards of his time. Do not repeat the myth as fact. See the conflicting-accounts memo.
- Cause of death. This is genuinely disputed. We must not state a single cause as settled. Again, see the memo.
- Waterloo weather. The story that the rain lost him the battle is popular but contested. Present it as one theory, not the theory.

Tone: sympathetic but not worshipful. He was a reformer and a tyrant, often in the same week. The Napoleonic Code and the reintroduction of colonial slavery both belong to him.

Strongest primary-adjacent sources so far: the Marchetti interview (excellent for quotes), the Waterloo retrospective feature (good for the climax), and the biographical overview for the fact spine. Weakest area: firsthand Saint Helena material, which is thin and heavily colored by whoever wrote it.
