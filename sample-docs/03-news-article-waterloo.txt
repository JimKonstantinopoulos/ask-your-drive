ARCHIVE ARTICLE (retrospective feature)
"The Field That Ended an Empire: Waterloo, 200 Years On"
Collected as a secondary source for the documentary project.

On 18 June 1815, near a Belgian village most of Europe had never heard of, the Napoleonic era ended in a single day of fighting. The Battle of Waterloo pitted Napoleon's roughly 73,000 troops against the Duke of Wellington's Anglo-allied army and, decisively, the Prussian forces under Blücher who arrived in the afternoon.

Rain the night before had turned the ground to mud. Napoleon delayed his main attack for hours to let the field dry, a delay many historians argue cost him the battle by giving the Prussians time to reach it. By evening his Imperial Guard, which had never been broken, broke. The retreat became a rout.

Wellington famously called it "the nearest-run thing you ever saw in your life." The human cost was staggering: tens of thousands killed or wounded on a front only a few kilometers wide.

Waterloo did more than end a battle. It ended Napoleon's second reign after just over a hundred days, sent him to permanent exile on Saint Helena, and gave the English language a phrase, "to meet your Waterloo," that outlived every general who fought there. For our purposes, Waterloo is the natural climax of the film. Every earlier triumph should be shot with this ending in mind.
