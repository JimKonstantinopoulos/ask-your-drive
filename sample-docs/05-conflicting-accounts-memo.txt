MEMO: CONFLICTING ACCOUNTS
For the documentary project. Read before locking narration.

This file exists because several "facts" about Napoleon are actually open disputes. Do not let the script state any of these as settled.

1. HIS HEIGHT
- Account A: He was unusually short, roughly five feet two. This is the popular image and the source of the "Napoleon complex."
- Account B: He was about five feet six or seven, average or slightly above for a Frenchman of his era. The confusion likely comes from a difference between French and English measurement units of the time, and from British wartime propaganda that mocked him as small.
- Our position: Account B is far better supported. Treat the "short" story as a myth we debunk, not a fact.

2. CAUSE OF DEATH (Saint Helena, 1821)
- Account A: He died of stomach cancer, which also killed his father. This is the mainstream medical conclusion, supported by the autopsy.
- Account B: He was poisoned with arsenic, whether deliberately or from arsenic-laced wallpaper pigment. High arsenic levels were later found in samples of his hair.
- Our position: Present both. The cancer explanation is stronger, but the arsenic finding is real and unexplained, so we do not slam the door.

3. WATERLOO AND THE RAIN
- Account A: The overnight rain forced a delay that let the Prussians arrive, and so the weather lost the battle.
- Account B: The delay was minor and Napoleon was already outmatched by allied coordination and his own poor health that day.
- Our position: Offer both as competing explanations. Do not crown a winner on camera.

If narration ever needs a single honest line about all this, use: "Napoleon is one of the best-documented men in history, and historians still cannot agree on how tall he was or how he died."
