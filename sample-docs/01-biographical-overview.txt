BIOGRAPHICAL OVERVIEW: NAPOLEON BONAPARTE
Research file, prepared for the documentary project.

Napoleon Bonaparte was born on 15 August 1769 in Ajaccio, Corsica, a little over a year after France annexed the island from Genoa. His family were minor Corsican nobility of Italian origin, and Napoleon spoke Italian-accented French for most of his life.

He trained as an artillery officer and rose with extraordinary speed during the chaos of the French Revolution. By 1799 he had seized political power in the coup of 18 Brumaire, and in 1804 he crowned himself Emperor of the French. At his peak he controlled or dominated most of continental Europe.

Key facts worth highlighting for the film:
- He was a voracious reader and reformer, not only a general. The Napoleonic Code, his civil law reform, still underpins the legal systems of dozens of countries today.
- He commissioned the first systematic study of ancient Egypt during his 1798 campaign, which led directly to the discovery of the Rosetta Stone.
- He fought roughly 60 battles and lost only a handful, with his final defeat at Waterloo in 1815.
- He was exiled twice: first to the island of Elba in 1814, from which he escaped, and finally to the remote South Atlantic island of Saint Helena, where he died in 1821.

Napoleon remains one of the most written-about figures in history. That is both a gift and a problem for us: the volume of sources is enormous, and many of them flatly contradict one another. See the separate memo on conflicting accounts before we lock any narration.
