INTERVIEW TRANSCRIPT
Subject: Dr. Hélène Marchetti, historian, University of Edinburgh
Recorded for the documentary project. Lightly edited for clarity.

Q: People think they know Napoleon. What do they get wrong?

MARCHETTI: The caricature is that he was a tiny, angry man who wanted to conquer the world out of vanity. Almost none of that survives contact with the evidence. He was of average height for his time. And the ambition was real, but it sat on top of a genuine reforming mind. He once said, and I am quoting from memory, "I love power as a musician loves his violin." That line tells you everything. Power was an instrument to him, something to be played skillfully, not just hoarded.

Q: What is the moment that defines him for you?

MARCHETTI: Waterloo. Not because of the defeat itself, but because of what it revealed. At Waterloo in 1815 you see a man who had reshaped a continent finally undone, partly by weather, partly by his own health, partly by better coordination among his enemies. He reportedly said afterward that the battle was lost by "a want of luck." I find that revealing. He could not quite admit it was lost by anything else.

Q: Give me one line for the trailer.

MARCHETTI: Try this: he built an empire faster than anyone in modern history, and he lost it faster too. The whole tragedy is in that symmetry.

Q: Anything about the end?

MARCHETTI: Saint Helena is where the myth-making really begins. Even his death is contested. Read the memo your team put together. The sources do not agree, and honestly, they never will.
